# ROOM TONE — a 12" house EP

A self-initiated vinyl project. One idea, carried the whole way through, so
the portfolio page can show a system rather than a single sleeve.

---

## The idea

House music is a room before it is a genre — a floor, a pair of speakers, and
people arranged between them. The whole identity is that: **a plan view of one
room, drawn as a rectangle, with dots for whoever is in it.**

Nothing else. No photography, no texture, no gradients. A thin keyline
rectangle, a few filled dots, and type set small in one corner.

The system is the variation. Same room every time, different arrangement of
dots per track:

| Track | Arrangement | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Two dots, far apart, near the speakers | empty room, soundcheck |
| A2 | A loose scatter, centre of the floor | it's filling up |
| B1 | A dense cluster, off-centre | peak |
| B2 | One dot, by the door | last record |

Press those four as a strip on the back, and the EP tells its own night in
four diagrams. That is the project — not the sleeve, the sequence.

**Why it works for a portfolio:** it is legible at thumbnail size, it proves
you can build a system off a single constraint, and it gives you four pieces
of artwork out of one decision.

*Alternate theme if you want something warmer:* the same minimal treatment but
the rectangle is a **dancefloor tile grid**, and the dots are worn patches in
the floor. Same rules, more texture.

## The copy

Invent the label and the artist — a self-initiated project reads better with a
world around it than with a real catalogue number borrowed from someone else.

- **Artist:** FWA *(or any alias you prefer)*
- **Title:** ROOM TONE
- **Label:** SECONDS
- **Catalogue:** SEC‑004
- **Format:** 12" EP, 45 RPM

### Front

Keep it nearly empty. Bottom-left corner, four lines, small:

## Specs

Get these right — it is the difference between a mockup and a job.

- **Jacket:** 314 × 314 mm trim, 3 mm bleed, 5 mm safe margin
- **Centre label:** 100 mm diameter, 7.24 mm spindle hole, keep type 10 mm
  clear of the hole
- **Spine:** 3 mm if you show a gatefold; otherwise none
- **Colour:** CMYK, 300 dpi. The whole thing is one ink plus stock — if you
  want to be accurate, spec it as **1-colour black on uncoated**
- **Type:** one family, two sizes, no bold. A grotesque — Helvetica Now,
  Söhne, or the Archivo you already have. Track the small caps slightly loose.

What to actually make, in order:

1. Front sleeve
2. Back sleeve
3. Four track diagrams as standalone squares *(this is the hero image for the site)*
4. Two centre labels, A and B
5. Printed inner sleeve — one diagram, blown up to bleed
6. One-sheet / promo PDF for the label
7. Digital artwork, 3000 × 3000 px square crop
8. Three mockups: sleeve flat, record half-out of the sleeve, and the four
   diagrams as a grid
9. A short 10-second loop of the diagrams cutting between arrangements — you
   already shoot and animate, so this is the piece that makes it yours

# More projects worth adding

Ranked by what your portfolio is currently missing.

**1. An editorial system.** You have no long-form typography. A 16-page
zine or a magazine spread set — a real article, real hierarchy, pull quotes,
captions, folios. It is the fastest way to prove you can set text, not just
display type.

**2. A packaging range.** Three SKUs of one product that share a system and
differ by one variable — colour, or a number, or an icon. Dieline included.
Shows range thinking, which the Dunkin rebrand hints at but does not finish.

**3. A wayfinding or signage set.** Arrows, floor numbers, a pictogram family
for a venue. Pairs naturally with the Goosebumps work and photographs well.

**4. A type specimen for the Homer typeface.** You designed it — show it
properly. A printed specimen or a one-page site: character set, waterfall,
sizes, a page of it in use. Designers read this as seriousness.

**5. A record label identity, not just one sleeve.** If ROOM TONE goes well,
build SECONDS out: logo, sleeve template, four releases in the system, socials.
This is the single strongest portfolio piece you could own.

**6. A motion identity.** A logo that behaves — 5 seconds, a sting, a lower
third, an end card. You have the video skills and nobody else applying for
the same jobs has them.

**7. A poster series on a strict constraint.** Twelve posters, one rule, no
exceptions. Constraint series are the easiest thing for a stranger to
understand in ten seconds.
